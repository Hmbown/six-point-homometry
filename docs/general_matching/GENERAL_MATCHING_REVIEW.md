## 1. Multiplicities, arbitrary target groups and anchoring

The proof counts directed differences with **integer multiplicities**.
For a nonfixed inversion orbit `{g,-g}`, an unordered edge contributes one
copy to each element. For a fixed orbit, including zero or an element of
order two, it contributes two copies to the one element. Removing the
common `k` diagonal zero differences before dividing these multiplicities
by two is valid, including for repeated-coordinate tuples. Consequently
the signed bijection is exhaustive exactly as written. At an order-two
edge both signs satisfy the equation; restricting to one sign finds a
witness but does not exhaust all compatible universal presentations.

The universal group uses the lattice generated by the **rows** of `M`.
A coordinate column vector in a target abelian group is a homomorphism
from the ambient free group precisely when it annihilates those rows,
that is, `M v=0`. Thus the group presentation and the equation convention
agree. The proof does not require the target to be finite or torsion-free.

Separate anchor translations preserve the T/I orbit of each endpoint.
If a collision or a T/I equivalence occurred universally, any homomorphism
would preserve it. This proves the direction needed for a presentation
realizing a binary Z-pair. The reverse direction is correctly withheld.
For two anchored endpoints, an alleged T/I equivalence must send some
source point to target zero, giving precisely the `2k` candidates in the
draft. This also validates its embedding/inflation statement: a translation
equating two sets in a subgroup must lie in that subgroup. No unit action
or arbitrary group automorphism is silently included in T/I equivalence.

**Coefficient-field boundary.** The phrase "any abelian group" permits
additive groups of finite fields as coordinate targets. It does not replace
integer difference multiplicities by coefficients modulo a characteristic.
Equality of group-algebra autocorrelation over a field of positive
characteristic is weaker. For example, in `C2^3`, the four-point sets

```text
{000,100,010,110} and {000,100,010,001}
```

have different integer directed contents, while both autocorrelations are
zero over `F2`: every off-diagonal difference count is even and the
diagonal coefficient is four. The draft makes no false extension of this
kind; an explicit one-sentence reminder would help readers coming from the
prior spectral-factorization discussion. This counterexample is covered
by the independent regression, **[COMPUTED]**.

## 2. Every-rank torsion bound: the critical attack

The main potential gap was assuming that an arbitrary rank-sized minor
contained the full first block. The actual proof does not do that. Those
`k-1` columns are independent in the whole matrix because `B` contains an
anchor-star identity submatrix. A column basis extending this fixed
independent set exists: select further columns from the second block until
there are `r` independent columns. An independent `r`-column matrix has a
nonzero `r`-row minor. The selected rows are distinct first-copy edges,
giving a **simple** `r`-edge graph `H`. This basis-extension step works
without full rank or binary-realizability assumptions.

In that one nonzero minor, a Laplace expansion along the entire first
block has one possible nonzero term for each spanning tree of `H`.
The complementary determinant is a square minor of a signed/permuted
reduced incidence matrix with some columns omitted, and hence has absolute
value at most one. The incidence-minor induction is valid: after column
selection, a row still has at most two entries; if it has two their signs
are opposite. Tree minors have magnitude one, cyclic ones have dependent
rows, and disconnected choices have insufficient rank. Therefore

```text
0 < |det N| <= tau(H).
```

The lattice index needed here is the gcd of nonzero **rank-sized** minors,
not the determinant of an arbitrary square matrix or one selected minor.
To spell out the standard Smith-form input: unimodular row/column changes
preserve that gcd, by the minors' Cauchy–Binet formulas in both directions.
On a Smith diagonal matrix, the gcd is the product of its nonzero diagonal
invariants, which is exactly the finite torsion order. Thus `t` divides the
chosen nonzero determinant. One minor suffices for an upper bound even
though it would not determine the exact invariants. The proof uses this
distinction correctly.

The nonzero minor forces `H` to be connected. Adding unused first-copy
edges until `q_k=min(2k-2,m)` edges have been selected cannot remove trees.
This proves `t<=T_k` at every rank, with the low-cardinality edge cap
properly accounted for. If `r=k-1`, the selected graph is itself a tree,
so the chosen determinant is a unit and `t=1`.

The matrix-tree constant also survives scrutiny. The reduced Laplacian
cofactor is `tau(H)` by Cauchy–Binet. For a connected graph,
`adj(L)=tau(H)11^T`, so

```text
det(L+(1/k)11^T) = (1/k)1^T adj(L)1 = k tau(H).
```

The new eigenvalue in the constant direction is one; the remaining
eigenvalues are the positive Laplacian eigenvalues. Their sum is `2r`.
AM–GM therefore gives

```text
t <= tau(H) <= (2r/(k-1))^(k-1)/k <= 4^(k-1)/k.
```

Integer floors are valid. No full-rank-only assumption, dropped factor
`k`, or dependence on a numerical graph search occurs in this analytic
argument. The coarser arbitrary-minor bounds in Section 4 are also valid.

**[COMPUTED] Independent finite controls:** all incidence square minors

For each sample the test computes rational rank, gcd of every maximal

## 3. Low cardinalities and the positive-rank obstruction

For `k=2`, either sign identifies the two remaining generators up to sign,
so `U=Z`, and the endpoint pair is trivial. For `k=3`, the map from vertex
permutations to triangle-edge permutations is a bijection. Reanchoring
after that relabeling and reflecting one endpoint are integral unimodular
coordinate changes. The four remaining sign cases in the note are complete.
Their residual relations are zero or twice a primitive generator, so the
universal groups are exactly `Z^2` and `Z direct-sum C2`. None of the cases
divides an element of the target group by two. Each displayed equality or
inversion relation holds even when the target has two-torsion.

**[COMPUTED]** The independent maximal-minor enumeration finds exactly
12 triangle patterns of rank two/torsion one and 36 of rank three/torsion
two. Literal set enumeration on seven small abelian target groups checks

T/I fibre.

The four-point obstruction in Section 9 is correct. With edge order
`01,02,03,12,13,23`, its matching permutation is `(5,0,1,4,2,3)` and its
signs are `(1,1,1,1,-1,1)`. Eliminating the equations yields

```text
b=u, c=v, w=a+c, 2c=0, 2u-2a-c=0.
```

Setting `r=a,t=u-a` gives `c=2t` with precisely the remaining relation
`4t=0`; no extra relation on `r` survives. Its rank is five, its torsion
order is four and its universal group is `Z direct-sum C4`.
The height-zero fibre is antipodal and the height-one fibre is adjacent,
which prevents both translations and inversions between the displayed
endpoint sets. Both free projections nevertheless give the multiset
`(0,0,r,r)`. The stated map to `C12` preserves homometry and yields two
different T/I classes; the distinct cyclic gap multisets suffice, and the
immutable reference ICV and canonical-class functions independently agree.
These assertions are also directly regression-checked, **[COMPUTED]**.

This is a valid counterexample to deleting torsion and asserting a binary
or nontrivial free projection. It is not a counterexample to the exact
factorization through the *actual* target map in the next section.

## 4. Actual cyclic images and exact integer lifts

Although `T` may be noncyclic, its image in `Cn` is a cyclic subgroup of
order `q` dividing both `t` and `n`. Its identification with `Cq` via
multiplication by `n/q` is an isomorphism onto that subgroup. Selecting
integer lifts of the actual free-generator images therefore gives the
literal identity

```text
f(z,tau) = ell(z) + (n/q)chi(tau) = h(p(z,tau)).
```

The proof needs no disjointness between free and torsion images. A collision
or T/I equality in the intermediate cylinder would pass through `h` and
contradict the actual Z-pair. When `d=0`, the integer coordinate is zero;
when `q=1`, the pair lies in `Z`. Independent anchor translations can then
be restored exactly. For a noncyclic finite abelian target, retaining
`Z^d direct-sum f(T)` rather than claiming compression to `Z direct-sum Cq`
is the correct limitation.

For `gcd(n,t)=1`, a Bezout identity kills the entire torsion image. This
proves the noncolliding, T/I-distinct integer-shadow lift in the note.
The condition is sufficient for *one compatible matching*; it does not
require checking every possible matching. The prime-divisor cutoff follows
because no prime divisor of `t<=T_k` can divide such an `n`. Large modulus
alone is correctly not substituted for a prime-divisor condition.

The Smith convention in Section 8 is correct. For `PMQ=D`, the change of
solution coordinates is `v=Qz`, hence `z=Q^{-1}v`. Integer solutions have
the first `r` entries of `z` exactly zero, not just killed by the diagonal
invariant. Consequently their modular images are characterized precisely
by those first entries being zero modulo `n`; the free entries can be
lifted independently. The formula `w=Q(0,...,0,lifted free entries)^T`
then satisfies both `Mw=0` and `w=v mod n`.

**[COMPUTED]** A separate SymPy Smith decomposition of the four-point
matrix is checked with the full identity and both unimodular determinants.
The reviewer then tests 109 modular maps at `n=4,8,12,13`, including actual
torsion images and zero-torsion images when `gcd(n,t)>1`. The zero-coordinate
criterion agrees exactly with liftability, and every successful constructed
lift satisfies both integer equations and residue equality. This verifies
the direction of `Q`, rectangular rank convention and claimed successful
cases beyond the sufficient coprimality condition. It does not claim that
one failed matching proves a pair non-shadow.

## 5. Separate audit of the seven-vertex graph computation

The reviewer also read the complete C and Python sources,
`src/general_tree_bound.{c,py}`, the new graph tests and the dated results
README. The two traversals have independent counting mechanisms:

1. The graph-first method iterates every twelve-edge subset of the 21 edges
   of `K7`, computing its Laplacian cofactor by checked integer Bareiss
   elimination. The increasing-combination bit-mask successor and the
   prescribed iteration count exhaust all subsets.
2. The tree-first method decodes every base-seven sequence of length five
   by the standard smallest-leaf Prüfer rule, then adds that tree to each
   graph obtained by selecting six additional edges from its 15 missing
   edges. These are exactly its twelve-edge supergraphs. It performs no
   determinant computation, sampling or graph-isomorphism quotient.

The wrapper compares every table slot, including ineligible masks, and
then scans eligible masks with a separate Python combinations iterator.
It checks the total graph/tree incidence count and independently evaluates
the maximizing witness by rational elimination and direct acyclic subsets.
The persisted-result test constructs all 35 `K3,4` masks and compares them
with the entire set of maximizing masks, rather than inferring the graph
type from its degree sequence alone.

**[COMPUTED], freshly audited saved data:** the reviewer reran the complete
pointwise table comparison, without duplicating the underlying graph/tree
traversals. The tables agree at all 2,097,152 slots, covering 293,930 eligible
graphs. Their tree-incidence sum is 84,119,035; the maximum is 432 with
35 labelled maximizers. All recorded C/Python/test source hashes, raw

**Provenance hardening observation, not a GM proof gap.** In the audited
driver version, `--resume` checks the prior source/cardinality identity but
does not compare saved checkpoint bytes with the prior manifest's
`checkpoint_sha256` before intake. It subsequently replaces those digest
entries. Header/length/count-range rejection and the final independent
table comparison protect the recorded calculation against the tested
malformations and a discrepancy in one table, but they are not a blanket
rejection of every valid-shaped changed checkpoint. A changed header or
coordinated changed payload can be made self-consistent. The verified
retained archives do match the earlier recorded digests; no such change
was found here. Checking prior digests before resume would improve the
operational provenance contract. A complete checkpoint's processed header
also represents completed enumeration; a resume of that checkpoint skips
the traversal, so it must not be described as a fresh exhaustive rerun.
The author tests correctly compare unchanged bytes on complete resume and
the present reviewer distinguishes saved-data audit from fresh traversal.
