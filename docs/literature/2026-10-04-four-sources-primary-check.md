# Primary-source check of four historical sources (plus Goyette and Callender–Hall)

**Date:** 4 October 2026

## Summary

Access status and bearing on the claim that "no prior published statement of a complete six-point generating theorem for all cyclic Z_n, or of the repeated-distance six-point integer classification, was located":
**(1) Yovanof 1988 thesis — [READ PARTIAL]**: the full 355-page scan is viewable (not downloadable without login) in the USC Digital Library viewer at DOI 10.25549/usctheses-c3-279207; I read the Contents, Abstract (p. xiii), §2.7 (pp. 41–42), §4.1 (pp. 113–114), the start of §4.3 (p. 121) and §4.4 (pp. 152–157), §7.1 (p. 323). The thesis classifies only *spanning* six-mark rulers (all pairwise distances distinct); Family N is stated as the unique family under that hypothesis and the thesis's own synopsis says "the main problem remains still open". §2.7 defines cyclic spanning rulers and explicitly makes "no attempt" to pursue them. Nothing read undermines the claim; it confirms the collision-free scope.
**(2) Soderberg 1995 — [READ PARTIAL: p. 77 only, via JSTOR preview]**: p. 77 defines dual inversion informally and says the method is for Z_m generally, "when m>12" generating triples and quadruples; the formal construction (Theorems 3.20, 3.23, Q-grid definition p. 95) was read only through Goyette 2012's quotations (secondary). It is a construction/discovery method with no completeness claim (Goyette, and Peck's MTO review, both report that it does not cover all Z-relations). Nothing found undermines the claim.
**(3) Patterson 1944 — [ABSTRACT ONLY]** (APS abstract page read in Chrome; full text paywalled). Secondary descriptions (Goyette 2012 §2.1 with page citations; Jedrzejewski–Johnson 2013; Bullough 1961 p. 257) give the two complement theorems, a cyclotomic-set count table for n = 8..16 including numbers of homometric pairs/triples, a one-parameter tetrad family and an "m+2" family, and mod-16 six- and seven-point triples as examples only; Goyette states Patterson "does not provide mathematical derivations" for those. No general six-point theorem is reported. Nothing found undermines the claim.
**(4) Bullough 1961 and 1964 — [READ PARTIAL: first page (p. 257, resp. p. 295) each, via IUCr page-one image; remainder paywalled, US$40]**. Bullough I's own abstract says it sharpens Hosemann–Bagchi's subset-generation theorem and gives "a series of analogous and related theorems" with "very general pairs" and r-tuplets; its Theorems 3–9 (sufficient conditions) are on the unread pages. The block-translation statement "P ∪ T_x(Q) Z P ∪ T_{x+12k/mn}(Q)" is known only through Callender–Hall 2008 Cases 4a/4b (secondary, credited to Bullough) and Goyette's p. 263 citation of the tetrad formula. I could not verify the exact wording, hypotheses or any block-reflection statement in Bullough's text. This is the one residual gap: the unread Theorems 3–9 could in principle contain a general cyclic construction, but all secondary sources describe them as sufficient conditions and special families, none as a six-point classification. Nothing found undermines the claim.
**(5) Goyette 2012 — [READ PARTIAL: §§1.2.5–1.2.6, 2.1, 3.1–3.2 text-extracted]** from the UR Research PDF (sha256 matches the earlier audit); Formulas 3.2 and 3.5 recorded below. **Callender–Hall 2008 — [READ FULL]**; its actual title is "Crystallography and the structure of Z-related sets", not "Homometric Sets and Z-Related Chords".

Nothing located contains a complete six-point generating theorem for all cyclic Z_n or a repeated-distance six-point integer classification. Unread material (Bullough I pp. 258–268, Bullough II pp. 296–308, Patterson pp. 195–201, Soderberg pp. 78–100, most of Yovanof's 355 pages) means absence is bounded, not proven.

### What it states (verbatim where quoted)
- Abstract, p. xiii: "In 1939, S. Piccard claimed the proof of a theorem asserting the nonexistence of homometric structures among the members of a class of {0,1}-sequences whose unnormalized autocorrelation function is either 0 or 1 out-of-synch." ... "The proof of the existence of a unique, infinite, parametric family of counterexamples in the case of sequences of weight six completes the classification of all counterexamples to S. Piccard's 'theorem' up to that weight." (Autocorrelation ≤ 1 off-peak is exactly the all-distances-distinct / Golomb-ruler hypothesis.)
- §4.1, p. 113: "S. Piccard [145, p.31] presented a theorem in 1939 asserting that in the case where all elements in the difference set of a ruler are distinct, there is a unique ruler (except for Euclidean motions) that would generate it." Bloom's pair R={0,1,4,10,12,17}, S={0,1,8,11,13,17}; Bloom–Golomb families I and II displayed. p. 114: "In this chapter we completely classify all counterexamples to S.Piccard's theorem for spanning rulers with up to six marks. Specifically, we prove that the above two families can be expressed in terms of a single parametric family and, moreover, ... the only possible counterexamples to 'Piccard's Theorem' in the case of six-mark rulers are given by a single, infinite parametric family."
- §4.2 Lemma 2 (p. 115) and the whole difference-triangle argument assume "a pair of homometric, six-mark, spanning rulers R, S".
- §4.3, p. 121: "the general form for the difference triangles of two homometric, spanning rulers with six marks is the following" (triangles displayed); the proof proceeds by case analysis on min{b,c,d} with "backtrack Searching" (p. 152).
- §4.4, p. 152: "we proved that in the case of six-mark rulers is not possible to have more than two distinct homometric rulers." Families I, II (Bloom–Golomb), then search-derived Families 1, 2, 3 each shown to be linear transforms of I or II. p. 156, **Family N**: R = {0, a, 2a+b, 2b−a, 2b+a, 3b−a}, S = {0, a, b−2a, 2b−2a, 2b, 3b−a}, "a, b integers", with the explicit linear transformations to Family I ([u;v] = [1 0; −3 1]\[a;b\]) and Family II ([s;t] = [−2 1; 3 −1]\[a;b\]). This is the exponent set of the r, s polynomials in the question. The hypothesis throughout is spanning (distinct distances); the theorem is stated for integer rulers (line), not Z_n.
- §2.7, pp. 41–42: "It is fairly easy to generate cyclic (periodic) homometric ordered sets with difference sets consisted of all distinct elements based upon the theory of difference sets. We make no attempt to pursue the interesting topic of difference sets and combinatorial designs here." Defines "cyclic (periodic) ruler (modulo L)" and "cyclic spanning ruler to be a cyclic ruler with all distances distinct mod L"; cites Singer difference sets and multipliers as the construction. No cyclic classification.
- §7.1, p. 323: "Our main effort concentrated on the classification of all possible counterexamples to Piccard's theorem. Although substantial progress was made towards this objective the main problem remains still open." ... "Our main contribution to the subject is the complete classification of all counterexamples to S. Piccard's theorem for {0,1} sequences of Hamming weight up to six."

### Answer to the question
The thesis does **not** classify six-point homometric integer sets with repeated distances. Its uniqueness statement for Family N is explicitly under the spanning-ruler (all n(n−1)/2 distances distinct) hypothesis, for integer rulers, and it does not treat Z_n beyond defining cyclic spanning rulers. Not every page was read; a repeated-distance remark could exist in Ch. 3 or 5, but the Contents show no such section and the Abstract/Synopsis describe only the weight-six, autocorrelation-≤1 classification.

## 2. Stephen Soderberg, "Z-Related Sets as Dual Inversions" (JMT 1995) — [READ PARTIAL: p. 77]

### Bibliographic entry (verified via Crossref and JSTOR)
Stephen Soderberg, "Z-Related Sets as Dual Inversions," *Journal of Music Theory* **39**(1) (Spring 1995), 77–100. DOI [10.2307/843899](https://doi.org/10.2307/843899); JSTOR stable https://www.jstor.org/stable/843899 (Duke University Press). Note: Callender–Hall and Goyette cite the title in the singular ("Dual Inversion"); Crossref/JSTOR print the plural.

### Pages read
Page 77 (title page), in full, via the JSTOR free preview rendered in Chrome. Pages 78–100 inaccessible without institutional login or a JSTOR account (not created).

### What p. 77 states (verbatim)
"Any two sets A,B⊆Z_m are inversions of one another with respect to the index x if, for any e∈A, there exists an f∈B (and vice versa) such that e+f=x mod m. In this case the interval-class vectors of A and B will be identical."
"This paper extends the idea of simple inversion to a relation which will be referred to as 'dual inversion': under circumstances to be described (4.1–3), if some of the elements of A invert with respect to index x while the other elements of A invert with respect to index y, the resulting set B may be neither a simple transposition nor a simple inversion of A, but both sets may nonetheless share the same interval-class vector; that is, A and B may be Z-related."
"Furthermore, beyond Z-related pairs, when m>12 dual inversion can be used to generate (or at least discover) Z-related triples, quadruples, and, in principle, infinitely large collections of Z-related sets (5.4)."
"0.1 The distance, or interval, between any two elements e,f∈Z_m is d(e,f) = (f−e) mod m."

### The construction (secondary: Goyette 2012 §1.2.5, pp. 47–61, quoting Soderberg with page numbers)
- Cyclic collection Φ (Soderberg's notation; Goyette p. 51) and "H-sets" H = Φ ∪ I_xΦ.
- Soderberg Theorem 3.20 (p. 88, as quoted by Goyette): "Given H1 = (Φ) ∪ (I_xΦ) and H2 = (T_yΦ) ∪ (I_xT_yΦ), if A, B ⊆ H2, <A> = <B>, then V(A/H1) = V(B/H1)" (spanning vectors equal).
- Soderberg Theorem 3.23 (as quoted by Goyette, p. 55): "Given: H1 = (Φ) ∪ (I_wΦ), H1′ = (I_xΦ) ∪ (I_wI_xΦ), H2 = (T_yΦ) ∪ (I_wT_yΦ), H2′ = (I_xT_yΦ) ∪ (I_wI_xT_yΦ) with w−x = kϕ/2. For any A ⊆ H2 and B ⊆ H2′ with <A> = <B>, V((A∪B) ∪ H1) = V(I_w(A∪B) ∪ H1′)". Goyette: "A∪B inverts at I_w onto I_w(A∪B), while H1 inverts at I_x onto H1′."
- Q-grid formal definition (Soderberg p. 95, Goyette p. 58): a grid of cells, each the union of H_α and H_α′; Q is formed by choosing from each cell either H_α or H_α′, or A∪B with A⊆H_α, B⊆H_α′, |A|=|B|, or nothing; Q* ("Q-inversion" = dual inversion) applies I_x to the first kind of choice set and I_w to the second (Goyette pp. 59–60, citing Soderberg pp. 95–96). Worked example (Soderberg p. 96): Q={0,1,3,7}, Q*={5,6,9,11} with w=0, x=6, ϕ=12, Φ={0}.
- Generality: Goyette p. 47: "Much of his work is generalized to any modulus (not just mod12), and several of his examples explore these other microtonal universes." Goyette §1.2.6 (p. 61): "Soderberg's Q-grids successfully describe many z-related sets", but Goyette lists mod-12 pairs the Q-grid "do[es] not explain, as Soderberg observes". No completeness theorem is reported.
- Peck, MTO 9.3 (2003) conference review (https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.03.9.3/mto.03.9.3.peck_review.php), read: Soderberg's dual inversions "can account for Lewin's modulo 18 triples, as well as other n-tuples in spaces of even cardinalities. However, he pointed out that neither the GHT nor the dual inversion solution addresses all instances of Z-relatedness."
- Althuis–Göbel, Memorandum 1524, Univ. of Twente, April 2000 (full 9-page PDF read, https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/5115044/1524.pdf): §6 "The one-pitch shift is reminiscent of Soderberg's dual inversion (Soderberg). For, let the PC set P be the disjoint union of the sets A and B with A = {a}. Then the shift from a to b can be interpreted as an inversion w.r.t. x where x = a+b. Then I_y(I_xA ∪ B) = I_yI_xA ∪ I_yB. ... I_y(I_xA ∪ B) = I_xA ∪ I_yB, which has the appearance of a dual inversion." Their families: Property 1 (order 4, n=4m: [0,a,a+m,2m] Z [0,a,m,2m+a]); Properties 3–4 (order 5, n=2m); Property 2 (doubling A∪(A+t) when interval t is absent); Property 6 (order 5, n=5m); Property 7 (order m−2 in Z_{2m+1}: [0,1,4,6..m] Z [0,1,3..m−3,m+3], giving a six-point pair at n=17). They state (p. 5) "The two above properties certainly do not cover all Z-related pairs of order 5." No six-point classification.

### Answer
Dual inversion = inverting one sub-block of a set about index x and the complementary sub-block about index w, inside a "Q-grid" built from a cyclic collection; stated for general Z_m (p. 77 says "when m>12" for tuples), as a generative/discovery device with no completeness claim. Up to a global inversion this is a block translation; no block-reflection statement of the Goyette Formula 3.5 type and no six-point theorem were found in what was read.

## 3. A. L. Patterson, "Ambiguities in the X-Ray Analysis of Crystal Structures" (1944) — [ABSTRACT ONLY]

### Bibliographic entry (verified via Crossref and APS)
A. L. Patterson, "Ambiguities in the X-Ray Analysis of Crystal Structures," *Physical Review* **65**(5–6) (1 March 1944), 195–201. DOI [10.1103/PhysRev.65.195](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.65.195).

### Read
APS abstract page in Chrome (https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.65.195): "In this paper a large number of cases is presented in which two, and sometimes three or four, non-congruent sets of points are homometric, i.e., have the same vector distance set. Although the investigation is largely based on a discussion of one-dimensional cyclotomic sets, i.e., those in which the points divide the period on the line into rational fractions, it is shown that there are many families of non-cyclotomic pairs and multiplets and that each of these families has its counterpart in two and three dimensions." Full text: "Authorization Required" (paywall).

### Secondary descriptions (all marked secondary)
- Goyette 2012, §2.1 (thesis pp. 70–76): Patterson "defined the cyclotomic set as the equivalence class based on rotation and reflection"; "In one table, Patterson (1944, 196) listed the number of cyclotomic sets in mod8 through mod16 ... showing for example that in mod12 there are 224 cyclotomic sets" (Goyette's Example 2.1 reproduces the table, "reprinted in Buerger 1959, 44"); the table "also shows in the right-hand column the 'number of pairs' of homometric (z-related) sets, including as well the homometric triples and quadruples in mod16." Patterson "begins by considering 'a simple homometric pair'" in mod 8 (p. 197), generalises the tetrad in mod 1 with one parameter a, 0<a<1/4, and fixed intervals 1/4, 1/2 (p. 199; "Patterson cites the mathematician Paul Erdös as a source of the generalization"); and gives "a further generalization that combines some cyclic collection plus 2 additional elements" (m-gon plus a dyad whose offsets a, b sum to 1/(2m); p. 199). "Patterson also gives some examples of homometric triplets in mod16 of cardinalities 6 and 7. For these latter examples, however, Patterson does not provide mathematical derivations, but rather just shows the homometric triplets as examples of possible homometric groups."
- Jedrzejewski–Johnson, arXiv:1304.6608 (read, text-extracted), §1: "In 1944, Patterson [14] established the following two results: (1) If two subsets of a regular N-gon are homometric then their complements are. A Z_N B ⇔ A^c Z_N B^c. (2) Every N-point subset of a regular 2N-gon is homometric to its complement." §3: "Patterson [14] was the first to demonstrate the existence of t-uples of homometric sets. For N ≤ 12, homometric sets are well known." Their own table gives, for k=6: N=12:15, 13:2, 14:6, 15:25, 16:31 (28 pairs + 3 triplets), 17:16, 18:62, 19:21, 20:98, 21:96, 22:60, 23:33, 24:275; "The first triple appears for N = 16 and length 6: {0,1,2,4,6,9}, {0,1,2,4,9,14}, {0,1,3,5,7,8}". These counts are JJ's, not Patterson's.
- Bullough 1961, p. 257 (primary, read): "Patterson (1944) was the first to demonstrate the existence of triplets, quadruplets, etc. of distinct point sets which yield the same weighted vector set; he calls such pairs, triplets, etc. homometric pairs, triplets, etc."
- Callender–Hall 2008 handout does not cite Patterson by name (it cites Bullough 1961 and Rosenblatt 1984).

### Answer
Patterson's two complementation theorems are as stated above (secondary wording). He tabulates cyclotomic sets and the number of homometric pairs/triples/quadruples for n = 8..16 (per Goyette; I did not see the table) and exhibits mod-16 six- and seven-point triples as examples, plus a one-parameter tetrad family and an "m-gon + dyad" family. No general six-point family or theorem is reported by any secondary source. Full text unread, so this is bounded.

### Bibliographic entries (verified via Crossref and IUCr pages)
- R. K. Bullough, "On homometric sets. I. Some general theorems," *Acta Crystallographica* **14**(3) (1961), 257–268. DOI [10.1107/S0365110X61000838](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0365110X61000838). ### What was read (first-page image on journals.iucr.org/paper?S0365110X61000838 and ...64000718, zoomed in Chrome)
Bullough I, abstract (verbatim): "Hosemann & Bagchi (1954) have shown that it is possible to generate homometric point sets from subsets of points. Their theorem is here sharpened and a series of analogous and related theorems are given. The theorems are illustrated by several examples, and the properties of one of these are such as to merit a special investigation of this type of homometric pair. Some very general pairs are so discovered, and from these it is possible to derive explicitly sets of homometric r-tuplets (r > 2). Various preliminary definitions and theorems are given as a basis for later work."
p. 257, "Some definitions": sets S = S(0, x_1, …, x_{N−1}; z_0, …, z_{N−1}) with integral weights z_i, (z_0,…,z_{N−1}) = 1 (irrational weights excluded); convolution (3); vector set = S_1 S̄_1; "A necessary condition that two sets, S_1 and S_2, be homometric is S_1S̄_1 = S_2S̄_2. This condition is unfortunately not sufficient. Some sufficient conditions are developed below, notably Theorems 3–9. It has not proved possible to find useful conditions which are both necessary and sufficient." Also: "It is necessary to distinguish between three cases in which S_1 and S_2 yield the same weighted vector set." (continues on p. 258, unread). Sets may be periodic ("periodic sets are representable by periodic δ-functions of period unity") or non-periodic, in m dimensions.
Bullough II, abstract (verbatim, assembled from two zooms): "The theorem that sets which have the same weighted vector set continue to do so when subjected to the same non-singular affine transformation is extended to singular transformations, but it does not follow that homometric sets (h.s.) necessarily remain homometric under singular transformations although they may do so. It is also shown that sets which are not homometric may become homometric under singular transformations. The singular transformation of periodic sets offers special difficulties. It is shown that 'almost all' singular transformations of periodic sets do not exist. The (enumerable) infinity of transformations which do exist can be a source of new h.s., but the recognition of distinct homometric pairs (h.p.) is usually not simple. It is shown that every h.s. is a degenerate example of a larger h.s. and this occasions a re-examination of the definition of distinct h.s. An investigation is made of sets which become the 4-point pair Π_4 of part I under the same particular singular transformation. An argument which seems to be fairly general is used to prove that two 5-point pairs with this property are distinct but they are only two of many sets which transform to Π_4. It is shown that an (enumerable) infinity of distinct h.s. can be reduced to Π_4. It is suggested that h.s. obtained by singular transformations cannot be considered to be examples of their generating sets as are h.s. obtained by non-singular transformations. A group property of h.s. obtained by singular transformations is touched upon." §1 Introduction: "In an earlier paper (Bullough, 1961), hereafter referred to as I, it was shown (I, Lemma 1) that if two periodic or non-periodic point sets were homometric to each other they remained homometric when each was subjected to the same affine transformation Tx = Ax + c ... h.p. are then invariant under the full affine group."

### Secondary descriptions of the theorems on the unread pages
- Callender–Hall 2008 handout (primary handout, read in full), p. 3: "Case 4a (Bullough) ... Let P be the m-fold division of the octave that contains {0}, P = {12j/m}_{j=0}^{m−1}. Divide the smallest interval of P into n parts, Q = {12k/(mn)}_{k=0}^{n−1}, and transpose this by an arbitrary interval, T_x(Q). Then P ∪ T_x(Q) Z P ∪ T_{x+12k/(mn)}(Q)." "Case 4b (Bullough) This is the same as Case 4a with the exception that members of Q are not necessarily equal to 12k/(mn). Rather, each member of Q, q_k, belongs to the m-fold division of the octave that contains 12k/(mn). That is, Q = {q_k}_{k=0}^{n−1}, where q_k ≡ 12k/(mn) mod 12/m. With this modification, the result in Case 4a also obtains." Also p. 2, "Case 1 (Bullough, Rosenblatt) {0, x, x+3, 6} Z {0, x+3, 6, x+6}". The handout does not give Bullough's theorem numbers.
- Goyette 2012, p. 73–74: "Bullough 1961 also gives particular attention to this pair, which he considers to be 'an interesting homometric pair' (263)"; Example 2.5 "Formula for the tetrachordal homometric pair (Bullough 1961, 263): S1 = (0, a, 1/4, 1/2+a), S2 = (0, a, 1/2+a, 3/4)". Goyette p. 119 credits his Formula 3.1 to Callender–Hall and says it "derives from Patterson 1944 and Bullough 1961". Goyette p. 96 (footnote): "There is no established symbol to indicate homometry. Bullough used the symbol" (continues, unread here).
- Jedrzejewski–Johnson 2013: "R.K. Bullough pointed out some general theorems [2]"; no theorem quoted.

### Answer
Bullough I's own abstract confirms it contains a sharpened Hosemann–Bagchi subset-generation theorem, "analogous and related theorems" (Theorems 3–9 are sufficient conditions), "very general pairs" and explicit r-tuplets; the sets are weighted, m-dimensional, periodic or not. The exact statement "P a regular m-gon, Q a refinement, P ∪ T_x(Q) homometric to P ∪ T_{x+c}(Q)" is attested only through Callender–Hall's Cases 4a/4b. I could not read Theorems 3–9 or confirm any block-reflection statement; those pages are paywalled (direct PDF URLs for a03047 returned 403 to curl; "Read article" leads to a US$40 purchase page). Bullough II is about affine/singular transformations and reduction of 5-point pairs to the 4-point pair Π_4; nothing on its first page concerns six points.

Formulas (printed pages; PDF page = printed + 17):
- Formula 3.1, p. 120: "{0, 6} ∪ x + {0, 3} Z {0, 6} ∪ −x + {0, 3}   x > 0 mod 3" (interpolated from Callender–Hall Case 1).
- **Formula 3.2, p. 120: "Φ ⨄ x + Ψ  Z  Φ ⨄ −x + Ψ"** — "Formula 3.2 holds true provided that Φ and Ψ satisfy certain conditions. The set Φ is a cyclic collection of cardinality m/ϕ that is generated by the cycle interval ϕ starting from {0} ... The remainder set Ψ is a non-cyclic collection of a particular formation such that the two transpositions (by ±x) of the set yields two identical spanning vectors (using Soderberg's terminology) in respect to the static cyclic collection." p. 125: "Given these four criteria for Ψ, Formula 3.2 produces all of the z-related pairs in mod12 that partition into one cyclic collection." Caveat, p. 123 n. 2: "Again, I cannot prove this claim, but instead am relying on empirically influenced intuitions." (Also p. 78 n. 13: "I cannot prove this claim, but of the examples that I have looked at in moduli 8-30, I have yet to find a counterexample.")
- Formula 3.3, p. 127: "Φ ⨄ x + Ψ Z x + Φ ⨄ Ψ"; Formula 3.4: "−x + Φ ⨄ Ψ Z x + Φ ⨄ Ψ".
- **Formula 3.5, p. 127: "Φ ⨄ x + Ψ  Z  Φ ⨄ x − Ψ"** — obtained by multiplying the right side of 3.2 by −1; "Formula 3.5 describes z-inversion (ZI) ... inverting the remainder set around x forms the z-related partner." "Any z-related pair that is expressible as the union of one cyclic collection and one remainder set can be conceived in terms of both z-transposition and z-inversion."

## Recommended citation entries (REFERENCES.md format)

- **G. S. Yovanof.** *Homometric Structures.* Ph.D. dissertation (Electrical Engineering), University of Southern California, August 1988; advisor S. W. Golomb. [USC Digital Library](https://doi.org/10.25549/usctheses-c3-279207). Read in the library's online viewer: Contents, Abstract p. xiii, §2.7 pp. 41–42, §4.1 pp. 113–114, §4.3 p. 121, §4.4 pp. 152–157, §7.1 p. 323. Family N (p. 156) is proved the unique family of six-mark *spanning* rulers (all distances distinct); the thesis defines cyclic spanning rulers but makes "no attempt" at a cyclic classification and treats no repeated-distance six-point case. Its synopsis states the general problem "remains still open".
- **Stephen Soderberg.** "Z-Related Sets as Dual Inversions." *Journal of Music Theory* **39**(1) (1995), 77–100. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/843899). Page 77 was read (JSTOR preview); pp. 78–100 inaccessible. Dual inversion inverts two sub-blocks about different indices inside a Q-grid built from a cyclic collection, for general Z_m; a generative method with no completeness claim. Theorems 3.20/3.23 and the Q-grid definition are known here only through Goyette 2012 §1.2.5.
- **A. L. Patterson.** "Ambiguities in the X-Ray Analysis of Crystal Structures." *Physical Review* **65**(5–6) (1944), 195–201. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.65.195). Abstract only; full text paywalled. The two complementation theorems, the cyclotomic-set table for n = 8..16 with pair/triple counts, the tetrad and "m-gon + dyad" families, and the mod-16 six- and seven-point triples (examples without derivation) are reported via Goyette 2012 §2.1, Jedrzejewski–Johnson 2013 and Bullough 1961 p. 257.
- **R. K. Bullough.** "On homometric sets. I. Some general theorems." *Acta Crystallographica* **14**(3) (1961), 257–268. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0365110X61000838). Page 257 (abstract and definitions) read on the publisher page; pp. 258–268 paywalled. Sharpens Hosemann–Bagchi's subset-generation theorem and gives sufficient conditions (Theorems 3–9) and explicit r-tuplets for weighted periodic/non-periodic sets. The block-translation Cases 4a/4b are attributed to this paper by Callender–Hall 2008 only.
- **R. K. Bullough.** "On homometric sets. II. Sets obtained by singular transformations." *Acta Crystallographica* **17**(3) (1964), 295–308. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0365110X64000718). Page 295 read; remainder paywalled. Affine/singular transformations of homometric sets and reduction of 5-point pairs to the 4-point pair Π_4; not a six-point source.
- **Jeremiah Goyette.** *The Z-Relation in Theory and Practice.* Ph.D. dissertation, University of Rochester (Eastman School of Music), 2012; advisor D. Headlam. [UR Research](http://hdl.handle.net/1802/24817). Read §§1.2.5–1.2.6, 2.1, 3.1–3.2 (printed pp. 47–61, 65–76, 118–128). Formula 3.2 (p. 120) Φ ⨄ (x+Ψ) Z Φ ⨄ (−x+Ψ) and Formula 3.5 (p. 127) Φ ⨄ (x+Ψ) Z Φ ⨄ (x−Ψ); the criteria on Ψ are stated empirically ("I cannot prove this claim", p. 123 n. 2).
- **Clifton Callender and Rachel Hall.** "Crystallography and the structure of Z-related sets." Handout, Society for Music Theory annual meeting, Nashville, 7 November 2008. [Author-hosted PDF](https://myweb.fsu.edu/ccallender/z-relationhandout.pdf). Read in full (4 pp.). Cases 1–4; Cases 4a/4b are credited to Bullough 1961 and stated in continuous pitch-class space.
- **T. A. Althuis and F. Göbel.** "Z-related pairs in microtonal systems." Memorandum No. 1524, Faculty of Mathematical Sciences, University of Twente, April 2000, ISSN 0169-2690. [Repository PDF](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/5115044/1524.pdf). Read in full (9 pp.). Properties 1–10; compares Soderberg's dual inversion with the one-pitch shift; no six-point classification.
- **A. Bekir and S. W. Golomb.** "There Are No Further Counterexamples to S. Piccard's Theorem." *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* **53**(8) (2007), 2864–2867. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2007.899468). Metadata and abstract verified (Crossref, OpenAlex); text unread. Golomb-ruler (distinct-distance) scope.
