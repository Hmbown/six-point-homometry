## Theorem T: three-dyad branch

Let A,B be six-element homometric configurations in a finitely generated
abelian group G. Suppose a homomorphism to a torsion-free group sends both
to the same multiset of three affinely independent heights, each with
multiplicity two. Thus, after translating the first height to zero, the
other two are linearly independent over Q. Fix any compatible signed-edge
matching, and form its separately anchored universal relation group.

Then that presentation has one of the following dispositions, up to
separate label changes, anchor translations, and interchanging A and B:

1. It forces two vertices within one tuple to coincide.
2. It makes the whole tuples T/I equivalent.
3. The two universal configurations are related, up to a global rigid
   alignment, by the existing periodic-cross-correlation translation L2
   or the existing order-two block translation L4.
4. Its universal group is C2 x C2 x Z^2, and one tuple contains, among its
   within-set differences, all three nonzero elements of a C2 x C2 subgroup.
   Every homomorphism to a cyclic group therefore causes a point collision.

Consequently, **every binary nontrivial cyclic realization in this branch
is generated by L2 or L4**. The theorem does not declare disposition 4
trivial in arbitrary abelian groups. It excludes it only as the source of
a cyclic six-element set. Some of the universal groups in disposition 3
have additional free directions; the statement does not require the
chosen height projection to be the entire universal free quotient.

## 1. Exhaustive signed matching space

Label each tuple in three fibres `(0,1),(2,3),(4,5)` at heights 0,u,v.
The three nonzero height differences, up to sign, are u,v,v-u. They are
nonzero and distinct by affine independence. Each cross-fibre bucket
contains four edge occurrences. With both tuples given the same ordered
fibre labels, each cross matching uses sign +1, and is any permutation
of those four occurrences. There are three such buckets.

The three within-fibre edges have zero height. They may be matched by any
permutation and either sign on each edge. Their actual group differences
need not vanish, so the negative signs cannot be dropped. The total is

    (4!)^3 * 3! * 2^3 = 663,552.

This is all compatible signed matchings, without a coordinate bound or a
numerical specialization argument. A tuple `(c0,c1,c2,t)` encodes the three
cross permutations in lexicographic S4 order and the signed internal S3
permutation. The code is `((c0*24+c1)*24+c2)*48+t`.

Every homometric pair admits such a signed matching: directed group
differences can be paired in classes `{g,-g}`, including involutions.
Projecting a matching to the independent heights forces exactly the bucket
structure above. Every original configuration is an image of its universal
group `Z^10 / row_Z(M)`.

## 2. Exact symmetry reduction

The following transformations preserve the matching problem and its
universal group/configuration up to the allowed isomorphisms:

- Swap the two labels separately in any of the three A fibres or three B
  fibres: the group `(C2)^6`.
- Apply the same permutation of the three fibres to A and B: S3.
- Interchange A and B: C2.

These generate a group of 64*6*2=768 transformations. When a permutation
moves the anchored vertex, subtract the new anchor from that tuple.
The induced change of the five anchored coordinates is integral and
invertible, since its inverse is another such relabelling/reanchoring.
Changing endpoint order changes a row sign, which does not change its
integer relation lattice. Interchanging A and B reverses the edge
bijection and preserves its signed equalities.

The builder computes the orbits using six individual point swaps, two
adjacent fibre permutations, and A/B interchange. Each generator acts
componentwise on the four finite code factors, with a permutation of the
three cross-factor positions where needed. The tables are derived from
actual relabelled edges, and their simultaneous-factor action is checked.

The independent checker instead directly applies all 768 actual vertex
transformations to each representative. Each resulting set equals the
saved orbit member list. These lists are disjoint and cover every integer
code from 0 through 663551 exactly once. Their minimum elements are the
saved representatives. Thus neither missing orbits nor accidentally
merging two orbits is left to a normal-form implementation.

There are exactly **1,495 symmetry orbits**. The reduction took 3.91 seconds
in the initial run. Universal groups for their representatives took 1.62
seconds; no 663,552-case Smith run was necessary.

For forced collisions, the certified coordinates identify two distinct
labels in the same tuple. For T/I cases, the certificate gives a sign and
B-anchor whose transformed six vertices equal A. Both conclusions survive
every homomorphism of the universal group.

For each of the eighteen mechanism cases, the file gives an aligned B,
the common subset C, the removed block W, its translate W+d, and d. These
are exact universal group identities, and the checker directly verifies
the structural condition:

**L2.** For `P=C*W~`, the certificate proves `x^d P=P`. Thus
`C*(W+d)~=x^-d P=P`, and its reflected cross term agrees too. Internal
autocorrelation of W is invariant under translation, so A and B have
equal autocorrelation.

**L4.** The certificate proves d is nonzero, 2d=0, and the symmetric
cross-correlation `Q=C*W~+W*C~` is fixed by x^d. Translating W by d
multiplies both cross terms by x^d because d=-d. Its internal
autocorrelation is again unchanged. This is precisely the existing L4
condition, rather than the tautological assertion that the endpoint pair
is homometric.

All conditions are checked as integer group-ring coefficient counters.
There are twelve L4 representatives in C4 x Z^2, two in C2 x C2 x Z^2,
and one in C2 x Z^3. The L2 representatives comprise one C4 x Z^2 and two
C2 x C2 x Z^2. Distinctness and T/I must still be checked after mapping
any of these templates to the desired cyclic group.

## 4. The two noncyclic residues

The remaining two matching orbits have the same displayed universal
configuration in C2 x C2 x Z^2. With e,f independent involutions and p,q
a free basis, it is

    A={0,f,q+e+f,q+f,p+f,p+e},
    B={0,e,q+e+f,q,p+f,p}.

The three dyad differences in A are f,e,e+f. They are distinct nonzero
involutions. Under any homomorphism to a cyclic group, the images of e
and f belong to its subgroup of elements killed by two, which has at
most two elements. At least one of e,f,e+f therefore maps to zero.
The corresponding two A vertices coincide. No choices of p or q avoid
this forced collision, and no coordinate bound is involved.

This proves the obstruction to a cyclic six-set without discarding the
nontrivial homometric configuration in its actual noncyclic universal
group. The saved obstruction certificate names the three within-set
differences; the checker verifies nonzero/distinctness, order two, and
the sum identity directly in the exact quotient.

## 5. Passing to cyclic realizations

The original cyclic coordinates factor through the universal presentation.
A universal collision would already collide in the cyclic image; a
universal rigid equivalence would already make the cyclic pair T/I
equivalent; the two C2-squared residues always collide by section 4.
Thus every genuine binary cyclic realization is one of the eighteen
certified mechanism cases. The universal rigid alignments and block
identities descend, as do the group-ring L2/L4 conditions.

Images must retain six distinct points on each side. This ensures the
displayed group-ring sums remain set indicators and preserves the
disjointness hypotheses of each move. A new T/I equivalence may arise in
an image; such an image is filtered out when listing Z-pairs. The result
does not merge T/I classes or identify points by a unit multiplication.

This completes the specified three-independent-dyad matching branch.
It does not address arithmetic-progression heights or other free-height
multiplicity patterns unless a separate reduction places them here.
