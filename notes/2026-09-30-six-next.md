Bloom six-pair locus at prime moduli. Fix the object counted as an unordered
pair of distinct T/I classes. Unit multiplication may organize the parameter

## Concrete next obstruction: local signs do not glue to global inversion

**[COMPUTED], exact counterexample, 30 Sep2026.** The prime theorem's two
moment invariants are not a complete invariant over composite cyclic rings,
even when every prime factor exceeds11 and both66 and162 are invertible.
At n=221=13·17 take v=(1,4), w=(118,30). Multiplication by t=118 sends
v to w and has t=1 mod13, t=−1 mod17, and t²=1 mod221, yet t≠±1
mod221. Both parameter images have six distinct entries per side:

| Parameter | X | Y |
|---|---|---|
| (1,4) | {0,1,2,6,8,11} | {0,1,6,7,9,11} |
| (118,30) | {0,15,45,60,118,193} | {0,45,118,163,178,193} |

Each row is a nontrivial homometric pair. Both have (s,e²)=(13,144)
mod221, but their unordered T/I pairs differ. An independent geometric
obstruction is the largest cyclic gap:210 for both first-row endpoints,
75 and73 for the second row. Translation and inversion preserve that
gap, so no first-row endpoint can be a second-row endpoint class.

The failure is structural. Since the coordinate configurations are linear,
X_w=tX_v and Y_w=tY_v. Any centered moment m_j scales by t^j; t²=1
therefore preserves every even centered moment and every squared odd
centered moment, not just the three used in the field proof. Treating these
different unit images as one class would silently change the T/I quotient.
The field root-separation step e(w)=±e(v) does not hold over this ring:
the local signs can differ. Prime-field orbit separation must be glued
with global point-matchings when seeking composite normal forms.

This is a boundary counterexample, not a composite counting theorem or a
claim that these particular two different pairs are homometric to each
other. Full certificate: `results/2026-09-30-six-prime-invariants/composite-obstruction.json`.
