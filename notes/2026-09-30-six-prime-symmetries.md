# Prime Bloom parameters: formal symmetries, collision lines and orbit count

This note makes no novelty claim. Sections 2–5 count parameter orbits;
§6 uses the parent's centered-moment argument, independently checked here,
to prove that those orbits are precisely the unordered pairs of distinct

## 1. Objects and the precise quotient

Let K=F_p, where p is a prime at least 13. For v=(a,b)^T define

\[
 X(v)=\{0,a,b-2a,2b-2a,2b,3b-a\},\qquad
 Y(v)=\{0,a,b+2a,2b-a,2b+a,3b-a\}.
\]

An admissible parameter means that each displayed list has six distinct
entries. The object associated to an admissible parameter is

\[
 \Phi(v)=\{[X(v)]_{T/I},[Y(v)]_{T/I}\},
\]

where each endpoint may be translated and inverted independently, and the
two endpoints are unordered. Multiplication by a general nonzero field
element is **not** part of this quotient. Inversion corresponds to the
specific multiplication by -1 and is already included in T/I.

The two coefficient configurations, represented as row vectors, are

\[
 P=\{(0,0),(1,0),(-2,1),(-2,2),(0,2),(-1,3)\},
\]
\[
 Q=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(-1,2),(1,2),(-1,3)\}.
\]

Thus X(v)=Pv and Y(v)=Qv. A **formal parameter symmetry** is an invertible
linear map M on parameters for which the corresponding equalities of
coefficient configurations hold, with a sign and translation vector for
each endpoint. This is stronger than an equality after projecting at one
particular parameter. The distinction matters: a formal-symmetry
classification alone does not rule out accidental projected equalities.

## 2. Twelve collision directions

Up to multiplication by -1, the fifteen point differences in X are

\[
 a,\ b-2a,\ 2b-2a,\ 2b,\ 3b-a,\ b-3a,\ 2b-3a,
\]
\[
 2b-a,\ 3b-2a,\ b,\ b+2a,\ 2b+a,\ 2a,\ b-a,\ b+a.
\]

The fifteen point differences in Y have the same multiset up to signs.
This also gives the direct formal identity proving homometry whenever the
two lists have distinct entries. Since 2 is nonzero in K, a collision is
equivalent to one of these twelve equations:

\[
 a=0,\quad b=0,\quad b-a=0,\quad b+a=0,\quad b-2a=0,
\quad b+2a=0,\quad b-3a=0,
\]
\[
 2b-a=0,\quad 2b+a=0,\quad 2b-3a=0,\quad 3b-a=0,
\quad 3b-2a=0.
\]

Equivalently, the forbidden projective slopes b/a are

\[
 D=\{\infty,0,1,-1,2,-2,3,1/2,-1/2,3/2,1/3,2/3\}.
\]

These are twelve distinct directions for every prime p>=13. For an
explicit arithmetic check, use the primitive row normals

\[
 (1,0),(0,1),(-1,1),(1,1),(-2,1),(2,1),(-3,1),
 (-1,2),(1,2),(-3,2),(-1,3),(-2,3).
\]

The nonzero absolute values of their pairwise determinants are exactly
{1,2,3,4,5,7,8}; none vanishes modulo such a prime. Every line has p-1
nonzero vectors, and two different lines meet only at zero. Consequently
the number of admissible parameters is exactly

\[
 |\Omega_p|=p^2-\bigl(1+12(p-1)\bigr)=(p-1)(p-11).
\]

There are no admissible parameters at any smaller prime. For p<6 the
ambient group has fewer than six points. At p=7 the twelve listed normals
cover all eight projective directions; at p=11 they cover all twelve.
This can also be checked by reducing the displayed slope list itself.

## 3. Explicit parameter transformations

Put

\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Z=-I.
\]

Thus R(a,b)=(-b,a-b), T(a,b)=(b,a), and Z(a,b)=(-a,-b).
The following identities hold as coefficient identities over the integers,
and therefore over every field. Here S+c denotes translation of a set.

| M | M(a,b) | X(Mv) | Y(Mv) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | (a,b) | X(v) | Y(v) |
| R | (-b,a-b) | X(v)+2a-2b | Y(v)+a-3b |
| R^2 | (b-a,-a) | X(v)-2b | Y(v)-2a-b |
| T | (b,a) | Y(v)+a-2b | X(v)+2a-b |
| RT | (-a,b-a) | Y(v)-a | X(v)-a |
| R^2T | (a-b,-b) | Y(v)-a-2b | X(v)+a-3b |

For -M, negate every set and every translation on the right side. In
particular every one of the twelve maps

\[
 G=\{\pm I,\pm R,\pm R^2,\pm T,\pm RT,\pm R^2T\}
\]

preserves Phi and carries admissible parameters to admissible parameters.
The formulas explicitly allow different translations at the two endpoints.

Matrix multiplication gives

\[
 R^3=T^2=Z^2=I,\qquad TRT=R^{-1},\qquad ZR=RZ,\quad ZT=TZ.
\]

The six matrices generated by R and T are distinct and constitute S_3.
Their negatives are six more distinct matrices for p>=13. Hence

\[
 G\cong S_3\times C_2
\]

(also isomorphic to the dihedral group of order twelve). This statement
does not rely on quotienting by the full unit group of F_p.

## 4. All formal symmetries, with an elementary completeness proof

We first classify affine bijections between the **planar** coefficient
configurations P and Q. An affine map of row vectors has the form
z -> zN+t. Its centroid maps to the target centroid.

The centroids are

\[
 c_P=(-2/3,4/3),\qquad c_Q=(1/3,4/3).
\]

For either configuration, the centered covariance matrix is

\[
 C=\sum_{z\in P}(z-c_P)^T(z-c_P)
   =\sum_{z\in Q}(z-c_Q)^T(z-c_Q)
   ={11\over3}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix},
\]

with determinant 121/3. All denominators and the determinant are nonzero
for p>=13. Under an affine bijection, C transforms to N^TCN and the scalar
(z-c)C^{-1}(z-c)^T is preserved. Its values are 8/33 on an inner triple
and 14/33 on the other triple. These values are distinct because their
difference is 2/11.

For P, the inner points are indices (0,3,4), namely
{(0,0),(-2,2),(0,2)}; for Q they are indices (1,3,4), namely
{(1,0),(-1,2),(1,2)}. Both triples are noncollinear, their difference-basis
determinants being -4. Therefore an affine bijection is determined by one
of the six bijections of these inner triples.

For P -> P, the six inner-triple permutations give the following maps.
Target indices are written in the order of source indices (0,3,4).

| target indices | N | t | preserves outer triple? |
|---|---|---|---|
| (0,3,4) | I | (0,0) | yes |
| (0,4,3) | RT | (0,0) | no |
| (3,0,4) | T | (-2,2) | no |
| (3,4,0) | R | (-2,2) | yes |
| (4,0,3) | R^2 | (0,2) | yes |
| (4,3,0) | R^2T | (0,2) | no |

The correct outer triple is {(-2,1),(-1,3),(1,0)}. All three rejected
maps instead give {(-2,3),(-1,0),(1,1)}. These two triples remain distinct
modulo every p>=13: the unique point with first coordinate 1 has second
coordinate 0 in the first triple and 1 in the second. Their first-coordinate
values are distinct modulo those primes.

For P -> Q, the six possible maps are

| target indices | N | t | preserves outer triple? |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,3,4) | I | (1,0) | no |
| (1,4,3) | RT | (1,0) | yes |
| (3,1,4) | T | (-1,2) | yes |
| (3,4,1) | R | (-1,2) | no |
| (4,1,3) | R^2 | (1,2) | no |
| (4,3,1) | R^2T | (1,2) | yes |

The correct outer triple is {(-1,3),(0,0),(2,1)}; all rejected maps give
{(-1,1),(0,3),(2,0)}. Their unique points with first coordinate zero
have different second coordinates, so the same argument excludes equality.

Thus the linear parts of all affine maps P -> P are {I,R,R^2}, and
those of all affine maps P -> Q are {T,RT,R^2T}. The maps in the
opposite direction are their inverses. Conjugation by the P -> Q affine
bijection having linear part T identifies all Q -> Q affine bijections
with all P -> P affine bijections. Its effect on linear parts is
N -> T^{-1}NT, which permutes {I,R,R^2} because TRT=R^{-1}.
Thus the Q -> Q list is exactly {I,R,R^2}; the inverse P -> Q maps
give exactly the same three reflection matrices for Q -> P.

Now a formal T/I parameter transformation that preserves endpoint order
must have M=epsilon_X N_X=epsilon_Y N_Y with each N in {I,R,R^2}.
No matrix in this three-element set is the negative of another for
p>=13, so epsilon_X=epsilon_Y and M belongs to {±I,±R,±R^2}.
A formal transformation swapping the endpoints similarly belongs to
{±T,±RT,±R^2T}, again with the same sign at the two endpoints. All these
possibilities occur by §3. This proves the completeness of the twelve
formal parameter symmetries in the stated characteristic range.

The covariance argument excludes characteristic 11 as a method, although
there are no admissible parameters there anyway. It is not an assertion
of new formal symmetries in characteristic 11. No assertion about the
formal coefficient symmetry group in smaller characteristics is required.

## 5. Freeness and orbit count

We show that no nonidentity element of G fixes an admissible v.

For R and R^2, det(R-I)=det(R^2-I)=3, so their only fixed vector is
zero in characteristic p>=13. For -R and -R^2, det(R+I)=
det(R^2+I)=1, with the same conclusion. The fixed space of -I is zero
because 2 is invertible.

The remaining six involutions have the following fixed directions:

| matrix | fixed-line equation | slope b/a |
|---|---|---|
| T | b=a | 1 |
| -T | b=-a | -1 |
| RT | a=0 | infinity |
| -RT | a=2b | 1/2 |
| R^2T | b=0 | 0 |
| -R^2T | b=2a | 2 |

All six lines are excluded by §2. Thus G acts freely on Omega_p, and
every orbit has exactly twelve parameters. Consequently

\[
 \boxed{|\Omega_p/G|={(p-1)(p-11)\over12}\quad(p\ge13\text{ prime}).}
\]

This is a general proof by line arrangements and group action, not a fit
to a finite list of primes. The expression is automatically integral:
the free action already proves divisibility by twelve. Directly, every
prime p>=5 is ±1 modulo 6, so one factor is divisible by 3, and both
factors are even with at least one divisible by 4.

## 6. Moment separation: no projected accidents and no endpoint collapse

The formal symmetry classification does not on its own classify equalities
after one-dimensional projection. The following separate algebraic
argument does so without enumerating projected point permutations.

For a six-element set A in F_p put

\[
 \mu_A={1\over6}\sum_{x\in A}x,\qquad
 m_j(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\bigl(3(x-\mu_A)\bigr)^j.
\]

Translation preserves every m_j, and inversion multiplies it by (-1)^j.
Thus m_2,m_3^2 and m_6 are invariants of an individual T/I class. The
centering uses only denominators 2 and 3 because 3*mu_A=sum(A)/2.

Set

\[
 S=a^2-ab+b^2,\qquad E=ab(a-b),\qquad
 D=(a+b)(2a-b)(a-2b).
\]

The scaled centered lists for X and Y, obtained directly by subtracting
half the respective point sum from three times each point, are

\[
 (2a-4b,5a-4b,-4a-b,-4a+2b,2a+2b,-a+5b),
\]
\[
 (-a-4b,2a-4b,5a-b,-4a+2b,2a+2b,-4a+5b).
\]

Expanding the second, third and sixth powers gives the integer polynomial
identities

\[
 m_2(X)=m_2(Y)=66S,\qquad
 m_3(X)=6D-243E,\quad m_3(Y)=6D+243E,
\]
\[
 D^2+27E^2=4S^3,
\]
\[
 m_6(X)+m_6(Y)=47892S^3+12798E^2.
\]

For an explicit expansion check of the last identity, the coefficients
of a^6,a^5b,a^4b^2,a^3b^3,a^2b^4,ab^5,b^6 on both sides are
(47892,-143676,300150,-360840,300150,-143676,47892).
The cubic formulas also immediately give

\[
 C_3:=m_3(X)^2+m_3(Y)^2-288S^3=116154E^2,
\]
\[
 C_6:=m_6(X)+m_6(Y)-47892S^3=12798E^2.
\]

The coefficients factor as 116154=162*717 and 12798=162*79, and
118*79-13*717=1. Hence

\[
 \boxed{118C_6-13C_3=162E^2.}
\]

Suppose Phi(v)=Phi(w). The common m_2 recovers S at either parameter,
since 66 is invertible for every prime p>=13. The unordered sum of
squared cubic moments and sum of sixth moments then recovers C_3,C_6,
and the boxed identity recovers E^2 since 162 is invertible. This remains
valid in characteristics 79 and 239, where one of the two separate
coefficients would fail to recover E^2. It also remains valid when S=0.

Thus S(v)=S(w) and E(v)^2=E(w)^2. Since the characteristic is odd,
E(w)=E(v) or E(w)=-E(v). Replacing w by -w if necessary, which applies
an element of G, makes E equal while leaving S unchanged.

The three elements

\[
 (a,-b,b-a)
\]

are the multiset of roots of

\[
 z^3-Sz-E=(z-a)(z+b)(z-b+a).
\]

Equality of S,E therefore says that the root multisets agree. The map
R cyclically permutes these three entries, while -T transposes the first
two and fixes the third. These generate all permutations. Consequently
w lies in the G-orbit of v. This proves that no projected identification
joins distinct G-orbits. Uniqueness of polynomial factorization over the
field suffices; no assumption that S is nonzero or that a real ordering
exists is used.

For distinctness of the two endpoint T/I classes at one parameter,

\[
 m_3(X)^2-m_3(Y)^2=-5832DE.
\]

Every factor of E and D is nonzero on Omega_p: the corresponding zero
directions are a=0,b=0,b=a,b=-a,b=2a,b=a/2, all in the collision list.
The coefficient 5832 has no prime factors except 2 and 3. Thus the
squared cubic moments differ at every admissible parameter, so X(v)
and Y(v) are different T/I classes.

Combining orbit freeness, moment separation and endpoint distinctness,
the Bloom family represents exactly

\[
 \boxed{B(p)={(p-1)(p-11)\over12}}
\]

unordered pairs of distinct endpoint T/I classes for every prime p>=13.
For smaller primes it represents none. This conclusion counts only
Bloom pairs. Total six-point pair counts additionally require an
applicable prime specialization of the inherited complete generation
theorem; that completeness is not supplied merely by these invariants.

### 6.1. Scope over other fields

In fact all the formal symmetry, freeness and moment separation arguments
above hold over **any field of characteristic different from 2,3,11**,
including characteristic zero. In the covariance proof the only required
inverses come from 2,3,11; the rejected outer triples still differ in
characteristic 5 or 7. The group matrices remain distinct there, and the
fixed-space calculation uses only inverses of 2 and 3. The moment proof
uses inverses of 66 and 162. No additional assumption on the field or
on S is necessary.

For a finite field F_q of characteristic p not in {2,3,11}, let d_p be
the number of distinct collision lines after reducing the twelve primitive
normals. These lines are defined over the prime subfield and meet pairwise
only at zero over F_q. Then

\[
 |\Omega_q|=(q-1)(q+1-d_p),\qquad
 B(q)={(q-1)(q+1-d_p)\over12}.
\]

For p=5 the slopes reduce to all six directions of P^1(F_5), and for
p=7 to all eight directions of P^1(F_7). More explicitly, in the order
of the twelve normals in §2, their reduced slopes are

\[
 p=5:\ (\infty,0,1,4,2,3,3,3,2,4,2,4),
\]
\[
 p=7:\ (\infty,0,1,6,2,5,3,4,3,5,5,3).
\]

Thus d_5=6, d_7=8, and d_p=12 for p>=13. In particular

\[
 B(q)=\begin{cases}
 (q-1)(q-5)/12,&p=5,\\
 (q-1)(q-7)/12,&p=7,\\
 (q-1)(q-11)/12,&p\ge13.
 \end{cases}
\]

Here homometry means equality of the directed difference multisets in
the additive group of the field, and T/I means x -> epsilon*x+t with
epsilon in {1,-1}. This field extension does not assert completeness
of all homometric six-point pairs in noncyclic additive groups. The
integrating proof is [the parent's prime/field theorem](2026-09-30-six-prime-theorem.md).

## 7. Single-endpoint rigidity and the genuine characteristic-31 exception

**Status: [PROVED], in-house, after the separately logged attack.**
This corollary was proposed by the parent task and independently verified
here by exact polynomial expansion and the concrete characteristic-31
witness below.

Expanding the individual moments, rather than their unordered sums, gives

\[
 m_6(X)=23946S^3+6399E^2-4860DE,
\]
\[
 m_6(Y)=23946S^3+6399E^2+4860DE,
\]
\[
 m_3(X)^2=144S^3+58077E^2-2916DE,
\]
\[
 m_3(Y)^2=144S^3+58077E^2+2916DE.
\]

For an endpoint A=X(v) define H=DE; for an endpoint A=Y(v) define
H=-DE. Both cases obey exactly the same linear system

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}m_3(A)^2-144S^3\\m_6(A)-23946S^3\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}58077&-2916\\6399&-4860\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}E^2\\H\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The determinant is

\[
 58077(-4860)-(-2916)6399
 =-263594736=-2^4\,3^{12}\,31.
\]

Over any field of characteristic outside {2,3,11,31}, the individual
T/I-class invariants m_2,m_3^2,m_6 therefore recover S,E^2. If one
endpoint of Phi(v) is T/I equivalent to one endpoint of Phi(w), this
recovers S(v)=S(w), E(v)^2=E(w)^2 regardless of which endpoint type
occurs at either parameter. The cubic-root argument in §6 then places
v,w in the same G-orbit, so Phi(v)=Phi(w). Thus **two different Bloom
pairs cannot share any endpoint T/I class** in those characteristics.
This conclusion permits S=0 and does not assume a real ordering.

In particular, in the applicable prime range for the parent's inherited
completeness theorem, once every nontrivial six-point homometric relation
is known to be a Bloom pair, every maximal nontrivial six-point family
has exactly two classes. Without that completeness input the conclusion
only concerns overlap between Bloom pairs, and leaves other mechanisms
untouched.

### 7.1. Characteristic 31 actually admits shared endpoints

The excluded characteristic is necessary. Work in F_31 and put

\[
 A=\{0,1,3,8,12,18\},\quad
 B=\{0,1,3,10,14,26\},\quad
 C=\{0,1,4,10,12,17\}.
\]

At v=(2,12), the raw Bloom sets are

\[
 X(v)=\{0,2,3,8,20,24\},\qquad
 Y(v)=\{0,2,3,16,22,26\},
\]

and their reflected translates obey B=3-X(v), A=3-Y(v).
At w=(8,17), they are

\[
 X(w)=A,\qquad Y(w)=\{0,2,8,11,12,26\},\qquad C=12-Y(w).
\]

All displayed sets contain six different elements. The formal Bloom
homometry identity thus gives the two pairs {A,B} and {A,C}. They are
different unordered pairs of distinct T/I classes: the sorted multisets
of circular successive gaps of A,B,C respectively are

\[
 (1,2,4,5,6,13),\quad(1,2,4,5,7,12),\quad(1,2,3,5,6,14).
\]

Translation cyclically permutes the gap sequence and inversion reverses
it, so their different gap multisets already exclude any T/I equality.
The two pairs genuinely share the endpoint [A]. Their parameter invariants
are S(v)=S(w)=0, E(v)^2=2, E(w)^2=8 in F_31, explicitly exhibiting the
failure of single-endpoint recovery of E^2. This is a constructive
obstruction, not merely the singularity of a proposed calculation.

An independent check with the immutable reference implementation confirms
that A,B,C are already their dihedral canonical representatives and that
all three interval-class vectors have fifteen entries equal to one.
The saved exhaustive parameter fibers in
`results/2026-09-30-six-prime-count/p31.json` contain the two stated
pairs with the stated parameter representatives. The explicit identities
and gap argument above provide the mathematical witness without requiring
those computations.
